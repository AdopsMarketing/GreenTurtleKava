<h3>The kavalactones content</h3>
<p>The active compounds in kava responsible for the effects of the root are called kavalactones. A high-quality kava should have about a minimum of 4 to 6% kavalactone content. </p>
<h3>Purity</h3>
<p>The best kava drinks are from <a href="https://www.healthline.com/nutrition/kava-kava">kava roots</a> without stems or leaves. Ensure to check the product description and labels or ask the seller questions about its purity. </p>
<h3>Third-Party testing</h3>
<p>Third-party tests ensure your kava product is free from contaminants such as mold, pesticides, and heavy metals. Make sure to choose a kava product that has undergone third-party quality assurance and safety testing. An organic certification is a good quality indicator that a kava product is grown without harmful effects. Always be wary of red flags such as lack of transparency in sourcing or extremely cheap prices. Expert opinions and user testimonials are also crucial for gauging effectiveness and quality. </p>

<h3>2. Is kava legal in the United States?</h3>
<p>In the United States, kava is legal for personal consumption. Kava is <a href="https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/kava">classified as a dietary supplement</a>. However, it is not strictly regulated like prescription medications. However, if kava products are properly labelled and meet FDA requirements, they can be sold in the United States. </p>
<h3>3. How do I know if a kava is of good quality?</h3>
<p>The plant variety is the best way to know. Genuine kava is made from very specific varieties of kava plants, such as noble kava cultivars. They are a variety known for desirable effects and top quality. They don’t have the harmful compounds commonly found in lesser quality kava like two-day (tudei) varieties. </p>
